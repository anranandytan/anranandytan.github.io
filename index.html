About

Anran (Andy) Tan, FRM

Ph.D. Student in Economics

Duke University  ·  Department of Economics  ·  Durham, NC

I am an Economics PhD student at Duke University. My research interests lie at the intersection of financial econometrics, empirical asset pricing, and machine learning — with a focus on high-frequency data, realized volatility estimation, and generative models for market microstructure simulation.

Prior to Duke, I was a full-time Research Assistant at the Energy Policy Institute at the University of Chicago (EPIC-China), applying large-scale NLP, computer vision, and causal inference to questions in law and economics. I hold the FRM designation.

Research & Projects

Evaluating Realized Estimators via Controllable Diffusion Models
Field Paper  ·  Advisor: Prof. Andrew Patton  ·  Duke University  ·  Feb 2026 – Present
Using score-based diffusion models to synthesize tick-level order-flow data for market microstructure simulation. Extends the Patton (2011) loss-function framework to a generative-data setting to construct robust rankings of realized volatility estimators — including Realized Kernels, Bipower Variation, TSRV, and HAR-RV — across simulated liquidity regimes and extreme market conditions.
Ongoing Realized Volatility Diffusion Models High-Frequency Finance PyTorch
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning of LLMs: LoRA / Sparse LoRA / HiRA
Course Project  ·  Advisor: Prof. Vahid Tarokh  ·  Duke University  ·  Oct – Dec 2025
Systematic benchmarking of three PEFT methods on DistilBERT across classification and language modeling tasks. Sensitivity analysis on rank, scaling factor, and batch size; identified HiRA as consistently superior in balancing gradient update smoothing across diverse data distributions and hyperparameter regimes.
Deep Learning LLM PEFT PyTorch
Naming and Pricing Strategy in Chinese Online Used Book Markets
Graduation Thesis  ·  Advisor: Prof. Xi Weng  ·  Peking University  ·  2021
Applied KDE and nonparametric regression to a scraped dataset from Kongfz.com, documenting a positive correlation between listing name complexity and price dispersion. Extended the Crawford-Sobel (1982) cheap talk framework to derive equilibrium signaling strategies across seller types.
